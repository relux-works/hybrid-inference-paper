# Smartphones for hybrid prefill (server) / decode (device): research notes, 2026-10-30

Tags: **[fact]** = URL + short verbatim quote; **[estimate]** = my arithmetic or reasoning, shown; **[unverified]** = secondary report, leak, or a page that could not be loaded. "not found" = searched, nothing usable. All pages were fetched on 2026-10-30. Byte sizes use KiB and MiB (powers of 2). bf16 means 2 bytes per element.

## 0. Headline numbers

- **iPhone RAM tiers.** 8 GB: 15 Pro/Pro Max, all of the 16 family, 16e, 17, 17e. 12 GB: iPhone Air, 17 Pro/Pro Max, 18 Pro/Pro Max, and the iPhone Duo (expected). No iPhone has 16 GB. Apple itself now restricts some on-device features to 12 GB devices (§1.1).
- **Active iPhones, late 2026 [estimate]:** about 1.5 B in total (range 1.4–1.65 B). About 0.55 B have 8 GB or more (0.50–0.60 B, roughly 37%). About 0.13 B have 12 GB (0.11–0.16 B, roughly 9%).
- **2026 memory crisis.** Smartphone memory costs are up about 350% year on year (IDC, Q3 2026). Q3 2026 shipments fell 22% (IDC) or 20% (Counterpoint). IDC expects the average selling price to rise 27.6% to $581. TrendForce expects 12 GB to be the high-end standard and 16 GB to fall back.
- **Small-model state per token (bf16, growing part only) [estimate from config.json]:** Gemma 4 E2B 6 KiB, Gemma 3n E2B/E4B 8 KiB, Qwen3.5-0.8B/2B 12 KiB, Gemma 4 E4B 16 KiB, Qwen3.5-4B/9B 32 KiB, Llama 3.2 3B 112 KiB, Phi-4-mini 128 KiB. The Qwen3.5 models add a fixed Gated DeltaNet state of 18 MiB (0.8B/2B) or 48 MiB (4B/9B) in fp32.
- **Measured phone prefill (Gemma 4 E2B, GPU):** iPhone 17 Pro 2,878 tok/s, Galaxy S26 Ultra 3,808 tok/s (Google LiteRT-LM). Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 NPU, Llama 3.2 3B: about 815–886 tok/s.
- **Thermal.** Sustained decode keeps only 38–67% of burst throughput after 600 s on an iPhone 17 Pro, and drops 44% from peak on an iPhone 16 Pro.
- **Network.** Ookla global median mobile download is about 110 Mbps with 24 ms latency; fixed is about 135 Mbps with 8 ms (Sep 2026 index). US 5G: 179–314 Mbps depending on operator and source. Korea 5G: 495 Mbps crowd-measured (best operator), 974 Mbps in the government drive test.

### 1.4 Estimates

- **[estimate] Total active iPhones, late 2026: ~1.5 B (1.4–1.65 B).** iPhones were 1.0/1.65 = 61% of active Apple devices in Jan 2021. Applying 61% to >2.5 B devices (Jan 2026) gives about 1.5 B. Asymco's ~1.6 B [unverified] sets the upper end.
- **[estimate] Active iPhones with ≥8 GB: ~0.55 B (0.50–0.60 B).** Shipments: >450 M through Q1'26, plus 55.1 M (Q2'26, Omdia), plus 57.3 M (Q3'26, IDC), plus about 20–25 M in Oct 2026 (assumed: a quarter to a third of a ~80 M Q4). That totals about 580–590 M by end of October, assuming ≥90% of post-Q1 shipments are 8 GB models (all current models on Apple's compatibility list have ≥8 GB). Subtracting about 5–12% for channel inventory and attrition gives ~0.50–0.56 B. Because 450 M is a floor, the upper end is 0.60 B. Share of all active iPhones: ~37% (30–43%).
- **[estimate] Active iPhones with 12 GB: ~0.13 B (0.11–0.16 B).** About 296 M iPhones shipped from mid-Sep 2025 to end-Oct 2026. That is ~20 M of iPhone 17 launch fill in Sep 2025 (assumed), plus 81.3 + 60.4 + 55.1 + 57.3 M, plus ~22 M in Oct 2026 (assumed). The 12 GB models (Air, 17 Pro/Pro Max, 18 Pro/Pro Max) are assumed to be 40–55% of these:
  - Counterpoint's "almost 10GB" Apple average in Dec 2025 implies 8 + 4x ≈ 9.8–10, so x ≈ 45–50% in peak season.
  - CIRP's US figure for the Dec quarter is 58%.
  - The share falls later in the cycle: the 17e launches, and the base 17 leads global sales in Q1 and Q2 2026.

  That gives 118–163 M shipped, minus about 5% inventory and attrition. Share of all active iPhones: ~9% (7–11%).
- Uncertainty is dominated by the total-base denominator (Apple has not published an iPhone count since 2021) and by the 12 GB mix after December 2025.

## 3. Apple Foundation Models

### 3.1 2024 (first Apple Intelligence on-device model, AFM-on-device)

- [fact] https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21075, Table 1: model dim 3072, head dim 128, "Num query heads" 24, "Num key/value heads" 8, "Num layers" 26, non-embedding params 2.58 B, embedding params 0.15 B. Vocabulary: "100k and 49k tokens for AFM-server and AFM-on-device". Quantization: "We choose to use 3.7 bpw in production"; mixed "2-bit quantization (default is 4-bit)".
- [fact] https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/introducing-apple-foundation-models (2024-06-10): "on iPhone 15 Pro we are able to reach time-to-first-token latency of about 0.6 millisecond per prompt token"; "and a generation rate of 30 tokens per second"; "Both the on-device and server models use grouped-query-attention."
- [estimate] 0.6 ms per token is about 1,670 prompt tok/s.

### 3.2 2025 Tech Report (arXiv 2507.13575)

Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13575v3

- [fact] On-device model, "∼3B-parameter on-device model optimized for Apple silicon". Layers are split into two blocks: "Block 1 contained 62.5% of the total transformer layers, while Block 2 contained the remaining 37.5%".
- [fact] KV-cache sharing: "Every key and value cache (KV cache) of Block 2 was directly shared with those generated by Block 1", "reducing KV cache memory usage by 37.5%", and the sharing is used to "reduce time-to-first-token (TTFT) by ∼37.5%". So Block 2 computes no keys or values during prefill.
- [fact] Quantization: "we compressed the on-device model to 2 bits-per-weight using Quantization-Aware-Training (QAT)"; "we quantized the embedding table to 4 bits per weight"; "We also quantized the KV-cache to 8 bits per weight".
- [fact] Vocabulary 100k → 150k; trained "at 16k sequence length".
- **Not published:** layer count, KV heads, head dim, hidden size.
- [fact] Server model is a PT-MoE (Parallel-Track MoE): "the PT-transformer partitions the model into multiple smaller transformers, referred to as tracks". It interleaves attention as "three local attention layers using a sliding window of size 4096" followed by "a global attention layer without positional embeddings (NoPE)", and is compressed to "3.56 bits-per-weight using Adaptive Scalable Texture Compression (ASTC)".
- Server parameter count: not stated. [unverified] Bloomberg (Nov 2025) put the current cloud model at 150 B.
- [fact] Context limits: "Apple's on-device foundation model has a context window of 4096 tokens per language model session" (TN3193, rev. 2026-03-31, https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/technotes/tn3193-managing-the-on-device-foundation-model-s-context-window.md). In the WWDC26 lab, the 4096-token window still holds and "PCC is 32 K" for developers (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/8121/).

### 3.3 2026: third generation (AFM 3), announced at WWDC 2026

Source: https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/introducing-third-generation-of-apple-foundation-models (2026-06-08)

- [fact] The family is "a family of five foundation models custom-built in collaboration with Google":
  - "AFM 3 Core, the next generation of our 3-billion-parameter dense model".
  - AFM 3 Core Advanced: "this 20-billion-parameter model uses a sparse architecture", "activating just 1 to 4 billion parameters at a time depending on the request". "the full model is stored in flash memory (NAND)"; "A lightweight, dense block selects a fixed set of experts during initial processing". Quantization: QAT, bits not given.
  - Server: "upgrades to the Parallel-Track Mixture-of-Experts (PT-MoE)". "we worked with Google and NVIDIA to extend Private Cloud Compute to NVIDIA GPUs in Google Cloud"; "AFM 3 Cloud Pro was optimized for NVIDIA GPUs".
  - A technical report was promised "later this summer". **Not found** as of 2026-10-30.
- [fact] Per-prompt expert routing: https://venturebeat.com/technology/on-device-ai-agents-hit-a-hard-memory-limit-apples-new-architecture-routes-around-it (Awni Hannun): "you do this once per query and then generate all the tokens with the same experts".
- [fact] Availability (secondary): https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/large-model-ai-for-apple-devices : "Available in fall 2026 with Apple operating system updates to Macs and iPhone 17 Pro/Max/Air phones". In the WWDC26 lab, Core Advanced is "used for the voice features". This matches the 12 GB footnotes in §1.1.

### 3.6 KV bytes per token for Apple's on-device model [estimate]

- 2024 dimensions (26 layers × 8 KV heads × 128 dims × K and V) give 104 KiB/token at 16-bit and 52 KiB/token at 8-bit.
- If the 2025 model kept similar per-layer dimensions, with 37.5% of layers sharing KV and an 8-bit KV cache, it would be about 32.5 KiB/token, or about 130 MiB at the 4096-token cap. **The 2025 and 2026 dimensions are unpublished.** The 2024 count of 26 layers cannot split exactly 62.5/37.5, so the dimensions changed.

- [fact] Qwen3.5 small models (released 2026-03-02) use the same Gated DeltaNet hybrid at a 3 linear : 1 full ratio. Every config has `"full_attention_interval": 4` and alternating `layer_types`; the 9B card confirms it. They also carry an MTP head (`"mtp_num_hidden_layers": 1`), which adds one attention layer of KV if used for speculative decoding.
- [fact] Gated DeltaNet state shape in HF transformers: "recurrent state tensor of shape [batch_size, num_v_heads, k_head_dim, v_head_dim]". Short-conv channels are `conv_dim = key_dim * 2 + value_dim` with kernel 4. https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/models/qwen3_5/modeling_qwen3_5.py
- [fact] Gemma KV sharing (Gemma 3n and Gemma 4): `first_kv_shared_layer_idx = num_hidden_layers - num_kv_shared_layers`. Shared layers reuse the K/V of the last non-shared layer of the same type, and "Layers sharing kv states don't need any weight matrices". https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/models/gemma4/modeling_gemma4.py
- [fact] In Gemma 4, full-attention layers use `{"head_dim": global_head_dim}` (512) (configuration_gemma4.py in the same repo). Google on Gemma 3n: "The keys and values of the middle layer from local and global attention are directly shared with all the top layers", "delivering a notable 2x improvement on prefill performance compared to Gemma 3 4B" (https://developers.googleblog.com/en/introducing-gemma-3n-developer-guide/, 2025-06-26).
- [fact] Gemma 4 releases (https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/releases):
  - 2026-03-31: "Release of Gemma 4 in E2B, E4B, 31B and 26B A4B sizes."
  - 2026-04-16: MTP variants for E2B, E4B, 31B and 26B A4B.
  - 2026-06-03: "Release of Gemma 4 12B Unified."
- [fact] Model card (https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-E2B-it): E2B "2.3B effective (5.1B with embeddings)", E4B "4.5B effective (8B with embeddings)". The card says global layers "feature unified Keys and Values", but the E2B and E4B configs set `attention_k_eq_v: false`.

## 6. Thermal throttling under sustained LLM load

- [fact] iPhone 17 Pro, Gemma 4 E2B, 600 s of continuous decode, unplugged. Method: "600 s continuous generation, cold (`nominal`) start, unplugged, tg128". Burst → sustained tok/s:
  - MLX/GPU: 48 → 18 (38% retained)
  - LiteRT-LM/GPU: 56 → 27 (48%)
  - CoreML/ANE: 33 → 22 (67%)

  Source: https://github.com/john-rocky/apple-silicon-llm-bench
- [fact] iPhone 16 Pro, Qwen2.5-1.5B 4-bit, MLX: "the iPhone 16 Pro loses nearly half its throughput within two iterations"; it then "enters a sustained Hot state (22.56±1.14 tok/s, −44.1% from peak)". https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23640v1 (2026-03-24). [estimate] At ≤1,000 tokens per iteration and 22–40 tok/s, 20 iterations take about 8–15 minutes.
- [fact] Galaxy S24 Ultra (SD 8 Gen 3), MLC-LLM, same paper: "The Android benchmark yielded only 6 iterations before the OS intervened."; "the Snapdragon 8 Gen 3's GPU frequency was floored by the Android thermal governor to 231 MHz"; "GPU temperature reached 78.3°C and CPU temperature 73.8°C".
- [fact] Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 on CPU: "By the 99th inference round, the frequency is nearly halved compared to its initial value." https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03613v5
- Not found: a sustained-**prefill** (prompt-processing) throttling curve on a 2025–26 phone. Google's LiteRT-LM tables do not state thermal state.

## 8. Energy: radio download vs on-device prefill

- [fact] Xu et al., "Understanding Operational 5G" (SIGCOMM'20, mid-band NSA):
  - "We find that the energy-per-bit of 5G is only 1/4 of 4G" (saturated iperf download).
  - The 5G module accounts for "55.18% on average of the total budget across 4 applications".
  - Radio tail: "4G returns to RRC_IDLE after about 10s", "while 5G takes about 20s to finish the tail stage".
  - Absolute µJ/bit values appear only in a figure; not extracted.

  Source: https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spring21/cos563/papers/5G_measurement.pdf
- [fact] Narayanan et al., "A Variegated Look at 5G in the Wild" (SIGCOMM'21):
  - "Over downlink (uplink), 5G can be 79% (74%) less energy-efficient than 4G at low throughput but up to 5× (2×) more energy-efficient when the throughput is high."
  - The downlink crossover is "187 Mbps and 189 Mbps" (mmWave vs 4G and vs low-band).
  - Absolute µJ/bit values are only in figures.

  Source: https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~fengqian/paper/5g_sigcomm21.pdf
- [fact] On-device prefill energy, SD 8 Elite Gen 5 (arXiv 2607.05475, Table 9, µJ/token, Xiaomi 17 / OnePlus 15):
  - Llama 3.2 3B: NPU GENIE 1.3e4 / 1.1e4; NPU MLLM 7.4e3 / 8.3e3; GPU llama.cpp 2.2e4 / 2.1e4; CPU llama.cpp 5.4e4 / 4.1e4.
  - Qwen2.5-1.5B: NPU GENIE 6.8e3 / 5.6e3.
  - Decode (Llama 3.2 3B): CPU llama.cpp 2.9e5 / 2.1e5; NPU GENIE 7.0e5 / 7.1e5.
  - In joules, 3B-class prefill is about **0.007–0.02 J/token on NPU or GPU** and **0.04–0.07 J/token on CPU**.
- [fact] iPhone 17 Pro, sustained Gemma 4 E2B decode: "LiteRT 0.147 vs MLX 0.182" J/token, measured "between gauge transitions" of the iOS battery gauge, which moves in 5% steps, so the precision is coarse (john-rocky repo).
- [fact] Older CPU data: "prefill energy consumption drops to its lowest point at 0.031 mAh per token" (Vivo Pad3 Pro tablet, CPU, arXiv 2410.03613). [estimate] At about 3.87 V that is ≈ 0.43 J/token.
- [estimate] Download energy per MB, from the device power range in the SIGCOMM'20/'21 figures (axes span roughly 2–8 W during transfers): energy per MB ≈ P/R.
  - 3 W at 1 Gbps ≈ 0.024 J/MB.
  - 4 W at 300 Mbps ≈ 0.11 J/MB.
  - 6 W at 100 Mbps ≈ 0.48 J/MB.

  On top of that comes a fixed tail cost per transfer (5G NSA tail ≈ 20 s; tail power not extracted). Shipping KV per prompt token would then cost:
  - Llama 3.2 3B (112 KiB/token, bf16): ≈ 0.003–0.055 J/token, the same order as on-device NPU/GPU prefill.
  - Gemma 4 E2B (6 KiB/token growing part): ≈ 0.0002–0.003 J/token.
  - The bounded sliding and recurrent parts are a one-off 6–48 MiB per handoff, ≈ 0.15–24 J.

State sizes are from §4.2 (bf16, no compression); link rates are 110 Mbps (global median), 190 Mbps (US median, all technologies) and 300 Mbps (US 5G). Prefill rates come from ~1K-token benchmarks (§5), so the on-device times are optimistic.
